2607.01319 What's the Matter with 3D Gravity?

by Robert Bourne, Jackson R. Fliss, Bob Knighton

Prerequisites

Virasoro TQFT, basic familiarity with Wilson spools.

Summary

Using Witten's phase space of AdS$_3$ gravity + minimally coupled scalar

$$T^*\big(\text{Met}(\Sigma) \times \Phi_\Sigma\big)\big/\text{Weyl}(\Sigma) \rtimes \text{Diff}(\Sigma) \tag{3.3}$$

they do geometric quantization, i.e. consider the Hilbert space of 3d gravity + matter as sections of a line bundle $\mathscr{L} = \mathcal{L}^k$ whose 1st Chern class $c_1$ equals the symplectic 2-form $\omega$ of the classical phase space

$$c_1(\mathcal{L}) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\big[\omega_{\text{WP}}\big] \tag{3.22}$$

Here $\Sigma$ is the initial time slice, $\text{Met}(\Sigma)$ the metrics on it and $\Phi_\Sigma$ the scalar configurations on it, and $k = \ell/16G_N$ with $c = 24k$ the Brown-Henneaux central charge.

Next note that the free scalar QFT can be rewritten as QM using the worldline formalism: trade the path integral over $\phi$ for a path integral over worldlines $x : \text{S}^1 \to M$ in the bulk three-manifold $M$, for a relativistic point particle of mass $m$

$$\int \mathcal{D}\phi\, e^{-S[g,\phi]} = \exp\left(\int \mathcal{D}x\, e^{-S_{\text{worldline}}[g,x]}\right) \tag{3.6}$$ $$S_{\text{worldline}}[g,x] = \frac{\mu}{\ell}\int d\tau, \qquad \mu = 1 + \sqrt{m^2\ell^2+1} \tag{3.7, 3.8}$$

So the geometrically quantized Hilbert space is the one on a slice $\Sigma$ with $n$ conical defects created by the “particles” corresponding to the scalar field via the WL formalism. Each defect has deficit angle $\theta = 8\pi G_N \mu/\ell$, and the $n$-particle phase space is built from

$$\mathcal{M}_{\text{grav},n} = \mathcal{T}_{\Sigma,n,\theta} \times \overline{\mathcal{T}}_{\Sigma,n,\theta} \tag{"3.13"}$$

Sections of $\mathscr{L}$ over $\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma,n,\theta}$ are $n$-point chiral conformal blocks with insertions of weight $h = \frac{c}{24}(1-\alpha_\theta^2) = \frac{\mu}{2} - \frac{G_N\mu^2}{\ell}$, where $\alpha_\theta = 1 - \theta/2\pi$; call the left-times-right space of these, before any MCG projection, $\mathcal{H}^0_{\text{grav},n}$.

Next, following the VTQFT paper for pure gravity, we have to gauge large diffs, i.e. mod out by the MCG, which is implemented via the projector

$$\frac{1}{|\text{Map}(\Sigma,n)|}\sum_{g \in \text{Map}(\Sigma,n)} g \tag{4.5}$$

Finally the total Hilbert space is a direct sum of the $n$-particle Hilbert spaces for all $n$

$$\mathcal{H}_{\text{grav}+\text{matter}} = \bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty} \mathcal{H}_{\text{grav},n}, \qquad \mathcal{H}_{\text{grav},n} \equiv \mathcal{H}^0_{\text{grav},n}\big/\text{Map}(\Sigma,n) \tag{3.27}$$

However if $n > n_{\text{crit}}$, then the conical deficits close up and we cross the black-hole threshold, which takes us to the non-perturbative in $G_N$ regime

$$n_{\text{crit}} = \left\lfloor \frac{2\pi}{\theta} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{\ell}{4G_N\mu} \right\rfloor \tag{5.4}$$

where the fused defect has weight exactly $c/24$. So for the perturbative Hilbert space we truncate at $n = n_{\text{crit}}$.

Naively the partition function diverges because the Hamiltonian vanishes as a constraint due to diff invariance, so $Z[\Sigma \times \text{S}^1] = \dim \mathcal{H}_{\text{grav}+\text{matter}}$, which is infinite once the Alekseev-Shatashvili boundary modes are there. But the boundary Hamiltonians are non-trivial, therefore they consider the partition function

$$Z(\tau,\bar\tau) = \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\text{grav}+\text{matter}}}\left(q^{L_0}\,\bar{q}^{\,\bar{L}_0}\right), \qquad q = e^{2\pi i \tau},\; \bar{q} = e^{-2\pi i \bar\tau} \tag{4.2}$$

with $L_0, \bar{L}_0$ the Virasoro generators dilating and rotating the boundary circle. This is evaluated via some algebraic geometry tools: there is this equivariant Euler characteristic

$$\chi(gq^{L_0}, \mathscr{L}) = \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} (-1)^i \operatorname{Tr}_{\text{H}^i(\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma,n,\theta},\,\mathscr{L})}\left(gq^{L_0}\right) \tag{4.6}$$

where all the terms except $i = 0$ vanish by Kodaira vanishing at large $k$, so the trace we actually want is $\chi$, and $\chi$ is computable by an index theorem.

Finally they have an expression for the full perturbative partition function on thermal AdS$_3$, which is the main result of the paper

$$Z_{\text{pert}}(\tau,\bar\tau) = |q|^{-c/12}\prod_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|1-q^m|^2}\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor \ell/4G_N\mu \rfloor}|q|^{2h(n)}\prod_{r=1}^{n}\sum_{\{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n\}}\frac{1}{\ell_r!\,r^{\ell_r}}\frac{1}{|1-q^r|^{2\ell_r}} \tag{4.18}$$

with the internal sum over partitions $\sum_{r=1}^{n} r\ell_r = n$, where $\ell_r$ counts the $r$-cycles of the permutation (not to be confused with the AdS radius $\ell$), and

$$2h(n) = n\mu - \frac{2n^2 G_N \mu^2}{\ell} \tag{4.24}$$

The $n^2$ correction is the new thing — it is the pairwise gravitational binding energy of the $n$ point particles, $\Delta E_n = -\binom{n}{2}\frac{4G_N\mu^2}{\ell}$, and Taylor expanding $|q|^{2h(n)}$ in $G_N$ generates the entire loop expansion. Dropping it, i.e. $|q|^{2h(n)} \to |q|^{n\mu}$, they compare to GMY and recover the known one-loop answer

$$Z_{\text{grav,1-loop}} = |q|^{-c/12}\prod_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|1-q^m|^2} \tag{4.26}$$ $$Z_{\text{scalar,1-loop}} = \prod_{l,\bar{l}=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{\mu/2+l}\,\bar{q}^{\,\mu/2+\bar{l}}}$$

They also do a non-perturbative resummation of this all-orders-in-$G_N$ series (will add more details later), finding that the first non-perturbative correction sits at order $|q|^{c/12} = e^{-\beta/8G_N}$, i.e. exactly at the BTZ threshold.
