1512.06109 Holographic Proof of the Quantum Null Energy Condition

by Jason Koeller, Stefan Leichenauer

Prerequisites (Grad level)

Basic familiarity with Ryu-Takayanagi, Fefferman-Graham expansion, comfort with basic differential geometry.

Summary

So in this paper, the authors are using AdS/CFT to derive the QNEC

$$\langle T_{kk} \rangle \geq \frac{\hbar}{2\pi} S''$$

for the boundary CFT, where $T_{kk} \equiv T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$, with $k^\mu$ a boundary null vector, and the derivatives on $S$ (i.e. $S'' \equiv \partial_\lambda^2 S$) are with respect to deformation along that same null direction, parameterized by $\lambda$.

The idea is that you draw an extremal surface and then you deform the entangling surface along a null direction in the boundary so that in response the extremal surface being anchored also gets deformed: so now you have a collection of extremal surfaces called $\mathcal{M}$. Entanglement-Wedge-Nesting (Wall's theorem from this paper) says that this surface is achronal so that a tangent vector $s^\mu$ to this surface is spacelike or null-like, $s^2 \geq 0$. When you choose the $s^\mu$ to be the tangent vector along the direction of deformation parameterized by $\lambda$ and perpendicular to the other directions in the tangent space, $\partial_\lambda X^i_{\perp}$, you get the QNEC.

Energy-Momentum $T_{kk}$ appears from the leading state dependent piece in the FG expansion when you try to compute the norm of the vector, and vN entropy $S$ appears because $X$ are the embedding coordinates of an extremal surface — themselves Fefferman-Graham expanded near the boundary — and its area is proportional to $S$ via Ryu-Takayanagi. Double derivatives appear because extremality is itself a first-order stationarity condition: the first variation of the surface (and hence of its area, i.e. of $S$) under the null deformation vanishes identically, so the leading non-trivial response of $S$ to the deformation is quadratic in $\lambda$, i.e. governed by $S''$ — matching the order of $T_{kk}$, which itself comes from a norm-squared and so is also quadratic. Other divergences that could have appeared from the boundary limit of the FG expansion are eliminated by choosing $k_i K^i_{ab} = 0$, where $K$ is the extrinsic curvature; it is exactly this condition that lets the FG expansion of $X$ simplify enough to close the computation.
